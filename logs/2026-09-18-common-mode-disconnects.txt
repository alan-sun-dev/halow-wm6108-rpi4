contains NOTHING in the ten minutes before the 09-15 event -- the first line is

===== what this establishes, and what it does not =====
ESTABLISHED: disconnects on this link come in two kinds, and the 2026-09-02
common-mode/local split applies to them as it did to beacon loss. Six disconnect
events in fifteen days: four seen by both boards in the same second, two seen by
one board only. Two chip revisions, two kernels, two rooms, one AP.

**And the polarity is the opposite of 2026-09-02's.** There, all 37 common-mode
beacon losses were harmless and every loss co-timed with a disconnect was local.
Here the common-mode events are the ones that cost both boards their link,
including the one that ended A2's 9-day unbroken run. Common-mode is not a
synonym for harmless; it is a statement about where the cause is, not how bad it
is.

NOT ESTABLISHED: the cause. The AP logged nothing before any of them, so an AP
software fault is not evidenced -- but nor is it excluded, because a transmitter
that stops radiating without saying so would look exactly like this. External
interference on the channel would look the same from here as well. Separating
those needs an instrument neither board has: a spectrum capture, or a third
station in a different position.

Also not established: whether `longest_outage_s` is still meaningful. It reads
426 s, unchanged since 08-27, because every outage since has been about ten
seconds. The 09-15 event took 9 s on A1 (21:44:29 -> 21:44:38) and 59 s on A2
(21:44:28 -> 21:45:27, including its own second disconnect).
