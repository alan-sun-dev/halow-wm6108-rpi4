Station 55:04 (Wio-WM6108 / MM6108A1 / SenseCAP M1 / 6.6.51+rpt-rpi-v8),
one floor above the AP. iperf 2.1.8 at both ends, 10 s per direction, TCP
unless stated. AP-side counters read immediately before and after each run.

THIS FILE WAS REWRITTEN AFTER ITS FIRST TWO CONCLUSIONS FAILED TO REPRODUCE
  The first version reported (a) "the AP fails 10.0% of its frames to this
  station" and (b) "the link is asymmetric by roughly 3x". Both came from one
  short measurement window. Seventeen further measurements put the failure
  rate between 0.0% and 1.2%, and a later window put the asymmetry at 1.53x.
  Both claims are withdrawn below. What replaced them is stronger.

WHY THIS IS THE FIRST REAL iperf ON THIS BOARD
  The 2026-08-26 morning table records 3.56 Mbit/s up / 5.34 down for this
  board "measured with iperf 2.1.8 between each station and the AP". The board
  had never had iperf installed -- see NOTES.md. Those numbers stand
  unexplained and cannot be compared with anything here. They are also
  asymmetric the OTHER way, downlink faster, which no run here reproduces.

  The outlier was also the slowest downlink ever recorded here, 1.63 Mbit/s
  against 2.15-2.67 for its neighbours -- anomalous on two axes at once. What
  happened during it is NOT KNOWN and no mechanism is claimed.

  Failures do not scale with load. From 1M offered to 10M -- far past
  saturation, since 10M delivers only 2.64 -- the rate stays 0.0-1.2% with no
  trend. The aggregation theory that motivated the ramp is unsupported.

  A rate under 1.2% cannot produce a 1.5x throughput ratio, let alone 3x.
  Frame loss was never the mechanism.

=================================================================
WHAT HOLDS UP
=================================================================
  Nothing is dropped in the driver. AP page_stats:
      Tx aged out 0     Page write fail 0    No page 0
      TX ps filtered 0  TX status dropped 0  TX dropped due to duty cycle 0
      Queue stop 1148   <- ordinary flow control under load

  The control still earns its place. In the same minutes as the outlier,
  dkmstest beside the AP ran tx_packets +9,239, tx_retries +678, tx_failed +0
  at 8.81 Mbit/s. Whatever the outlier was, it was not the AP's transmitter --
  a hypothesis this bench has already spent a day on once.

  The board is unharmed. Through ~150 MB of test traffic the association never
  dropped (14,129 s and counting), spi_errors 0, spi_timedout 0, dmesg
  failures 0, and the station's own tx_failed for the whole association is 3.

STILL OPEN
=================================================================
  Why does the AP pick an MCS one to two steps below the station's, when both
  radios report txpower 22.00 dBm and the two ends see each other within 2 dB?

  The AP is not choosing badly by its own evidence. State B mmrc:
      MCS4  40,135 / 45,227 attempts   88.7%   <- main rate
      MCS3   9,434 / 12,137            77.7%
      MCS2  13,733 / 14,781            92.9%
      MCS5 probe  26 / 275              9.5%   <- why it will not go higher

  With power and signal symmetric the difference must be at the receivers, and
  the tools run out there: `iw dev ... survey dump` returns NOTHING on both
  ends -- no noise floor, no channel-busy time. Verified by exit code, not
  assumed: exit 0 with empty output on the AP and on the station, i.e. the
  morse driver does not implement the survey callback.

  Next measurement, cheap and not yet taken: wait for the signal to drift back
  toward -52 dBm and re-run both directions. If the ratio returns to ~2.5x,
  "throughput ratio = PHY rate ratio" goes from two points to a relationship.
