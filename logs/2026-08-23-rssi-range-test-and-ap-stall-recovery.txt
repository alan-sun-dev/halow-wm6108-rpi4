PART 1 — RSSI SATURATION: CONFIRMED
===================================

Method
------

The station board was carried to three positions. The AP is the receiver
under test and stayed put, cabled to the laptop, so its reading is
available continuously regardless of what happens to the station's
management network. Both ends were sampled at 2 s intervals by loggers
running on the machines themselves.

Station power save was forced OFF for the whole measurement
(`iw dev wlan1 set power_save off`, re-asserted every 60 s by the logger).
This matters — see PART 3.

No transmit power was changed. `iw set txpower fixed` was not used; it is
what killed the AP's transmitter on an earlier attempt at this test.

Predictions were written down before each move. Free-space path loss at
923 MHz: FSPL(dB) = 20*log10(d_m) + 31.75, and the AP transmits at 22 dBm,
so predicted RX = 22 - FSPL.

Results
-------

  position                    predicted     AP-side measured        station-side
                                            (30 samples / 60 s)
  ------------------------    ----------    --------------------    ------------
  0.3 m, board to board        +0.7 dBm     -3   (range -2..-4)      0 dBm
  2 m, line of sight          -15.8 dBm     -15.7 (range -14..-19)   -12.1 dBm
  4 m, one wooden wall        -21.8 dBm     -29.9 (range -28..-32)   -27.1 dBm
                              + wall loss

Raw AP-side samples, 2 m line of sight:
  -19 -14 -18 -17 -14 -16 -17 -16 -16 -16 -19 -16 -15 -16 -14
  -15 -16 -14 -14 -16 -15 -16 -16 -15 -16 -16 -15 -15 -15 -14
  mean -15.7 dBm over 30 samples

Raw AP-side samples, 4 m through one wooden wall:
  -30 -30 -29 -30 -28 -29 -30 -29 -29 -29 -30 -29 -29 -29 -30
  -30 -31 -30 -30 -30 -32 -31 -31 -31 -30 -31 -30 -30 -30 -30
  mean -29.9 dBm over 30 samples

What this establishes
---------------------

1. The 2 m line-of-sight point lands 0.1 dB from the free-space prediction.
   The measurement path is accurate once the signal is inside the scale.

2. The 0.3 m point is clipped. The true value there is about +0.7 dBm,
   above the top of the scale, and the reported value sits at 0 to -4.
   The compression is visible in the step: true path loss from 0.3 m to
   2 m is 16.5 dB, while the reading moved only 11.7 dB (-4 -> -15.7).
   The near end is compressed by 4-5 dB, which is what a reading against
   its ceiling looks like.

3. `signal: 0 dBm` therefore means "off the top of the scale", confirmed
   by measurement rather than inferred. The earlier conclusion that the
   firmware does not populate RSSI was wrong, and the correction that
   replaced it (saturation, marked "not proven") is now proven.

4. The wooden wall costs 8 dB at 923 MHz. Two independent ways of
   computing it agree: absolute (-29.9 measured vs -21.8 free space =
   8.1 dB) and by the step (2 m -> 4 m is 6.02 dB of distance, the
   reading moved 14.2 dB, excess 8.2 dB).

Link quality was unaffected at every position: 20/20 pings, 0% loss,
RTT 8.3-8.8 ms at 4 m through the wall.

Margin, for orientation only: at -30 dBm with MCS0 sensitivity around
-95 dBm there is roughly 65 dB of headroom left. Any question of the form
"is this a signal problem?" asked with the boards on one bench was
unanswerable, and the answer at bench distance was always going to be no.

One thing NOT established
-------------------------

NOTES says a zero `avg_rssi` seeds the rate controller at MCS7 via
`MMRC_SHORT_RANGE_RSSI_LIMIT = -70`. At -30 dBm the AP's mmrc table shows
2 MHz LGI MCS0 selected — but that table had just been reset and every
attempt counter in it was 0, so this is its initial state, not evidence
that the seeding behaviour changed. Testing that needs its own design.


PART 2 — THE AP TRANSMITTER STALL: A RECOVERY LADDER
====================================================

The stall recurred on its own during this session, with no `iw set
txpower fixed` and no intervention of any kind — only read-only commands
had been issued. This is the third occurrence and the second that
followed nothing.

Diagnosis, by counter correlation
---------------------------------

Both readers were validated against a known-good case in the same breath
as the failing one, per the standing rule about empty measurements.

  test                                              result
  ------------------------------------------------  ------------------
  station sends 5 pings -> AP rx counter delta       +774 bytes   (up OK)
  AP sends 5 pings -> station rx counter delta       +0 bytes     (down dead)
  both sides idle 6 s, no traffic generated          +152 / +154  (readers live)

So: uplink delivers, downlink does not, and both counters are moving, so
neither reader is broken.

Host side shows nothing wrong. debugfs `page_stats` on the AP, 49 minutes
after boot:

  Beacon Tx: 29040          <- 49 min at 100 ms beacon = 29400. Beacons are
  Data Tx: 5659                being handed to the chip right up to the moment
  Page write fail: 0           of measurement.
  No page: 0
  Queue stop: 0
  Tx aged out: 0
  TX ps filtered: 0
  TX status invalid: 0

`dmesg` on the AP has not produced a line since second 16 of boot.
Thermal 46 C. `ip -s link` reports errors 0 dropped 0. The driver
believes it is transmitting and the host has no counter that disagrees.

The rate controller had barely been asked to do anything: for the
affected station, mmrc's whole table showed 2 total attempts.

The recovery ladder
-------------------

  rung                                  outcome
  -----------------------------------   ---------------------------------
  `wifi reload`                         does not recover  (recorded earlier)
  debugfs `restart`  (echo 1 >)         does not recover  (measured here)
  debugfs `reset`    (echo 1 >)         RECOVERS          (measured here)
  reboot                                recovers          (recorded earlier)

Paths, on this driver:
  /sys/kernel/debug/ieee80211/phy<N>/morse/{restart,reset}
The phy number changes when the driver re-initialises — it was phy0
before the bus reset and phy2 after. Find it, do not hardcode it.

What each rung does, from debug.c and mac.c in the 2.0.1 source:
  `restart` -> mors->recovery.driver_restart -> morse_mac_restart()
               reloads firmware, re-inits the MAC. On failure it escalates
               to a bus reset by itself.
  `reset`   -> mors->recovery.bus_reset -> morse_bus_reset()

`restart` completed cleanly and did not help:

  morse_spi spi0.0: morse_mac_restart: Restarting HW
  morse_spi spi0.0: Loaded firmware from morse/mm6108.bin, size 468304, crc32 0xbe7b5c8f
  morse_spi spi0.0: Loaded BCF from morse/bcf_fgh100mhaamd.bin, size 1251, crc32 0x941b2a82
  ieee80211 phy0: Hardware restart was requested

A full firmware reload over SPI is therefore not enough. That is worth
knowing on its own: whatever is stuck survives reloading the firmware
image into the chip.

`reset` recovered it completely:

  AP -> station   20/20, 0% loss, RTT min/avg/max 7.6/8.5/14.1 ms
  station -> AP   20/20, 0% loss, RTT min/avg/max 4.8/8.6/12.5 ms

and the link then stayed up for the remaining 18 minutes of continuous
1 Hz monitoring: 486 replies, 41 losses, and 40 of those 41 are the
deliberate power-off window while the board was carried to position 2.
One isolated loss at 13:48:25 is the only unexplained one.

A likely reason `wifi reload` cannot do it: this AP's `reset-gpios` flag
is 0, so RESET_N never fires on this board. Tearing down and recreating
the interface cannot put the chip through a hardware reset. The bus reset
path is what reaches it. This is the same device-tree property that made
the AP immune to the three SPI defects the station hit; here it works
against it.

Measured, not established: whether the stall's root cause is in the chip
or the RF front end. Everything host-side is clean, and receive keeps
working throughout, so the failure is downstream of anything the driver
can see.

A discriminator that turned out to be worthless
-----------------------------------------------

"AP cannot ping the Heltec either, therefore the failure is AP-wide" was
used during this diagnosis. It does not hold: the AP cannot ping the
Heltec at 10.41.0.197 even now, with the link healthy and the AP reading
its signal at -69..-76 and updating. The Heltec's IP layer is not
reachable for its own unrelated reasons.

What did carry weight, and still does, is radio-layer evidence: right
after the `restart`, the Heltec completed a full 4-way handshake
(authenticated -> associated -> AP-STA-CONNECTED -> EAPOL-4WAY-HS-COMPLETED)
in the AP's hostapd log, which requires the AP to transmit, while the
station's pings were still failing. Use the Heltec as a radio-layer
control — its RSSI reading in `station dump`, and its association events
— never as an IP-layer one.


PART 3 — STATION POWER SAVE MASKS THE STALL
===========================================

The station's power save is on by default: `iw dev wlan1 get power_save`
returns `on`, and the morse module's `enable_ps` parameter reads 2. The
AP is configured the other way: `enable_ps=0`,
`enable_dynamic_ps_offload=0`, `enable_twt=0`.

While the AP was stalled, the station's rx counter grew by 0 packets in
8 s — reading as "not even beacons are arriving". Setting

  iw dev wlan1 set power_save off

changed that to +154 packets in 8 s with no other change. The station had
been asleep; the absence of received frames was partly its own doing.

This matters twice over:

- For measurement. Any RSSI or loss figure taken with power save on is
  taken through a receiver that is off part of the time. The range test
  in PART 1 was run with it forced off for this reason.
- For diagnosis. It made an AP-side fault look worse than it was, and it
  is why the first read of this session's failure was "the transmitter is
  completely dead, beacons included". Beacons were going out the whole
  time.

Turning power save off did NOT restore the downlink — the unicast path
stayed dead until the bus reset. The two are independent.

The station/AP power-save mismatch is untested as a cause of anything.
It is a plausible contributor to the 5% packet loss recorded earlier and
deserves its own experiment.


HOW THE MEASUREMENT WAS RUN
===========================

AP side, /tmp/rssilog, 2 s cadence, both stations' signal plus our
station's inactive time. AP side also /tmp/pingmon, 1 Hz, recording
reachability of both stations.

Station side, a systemd unit installed for the test:

  /home/alan/halow-rssilog.sh          the logger
  /etc/systemd/system/halow-rssilog.service
  /home/alan/rssi-logs/rssi-<stamp>.log

It is enabled at boot on purpose: the board was carried out of management
range and power-cycled, and the requirement was that it keep recording
without anyone able to reach it. It also re-asserts `power_save off` every
60 s, because NetworkManager can turn it back on across a reconnect.

Remove it when the range work is finished:

  sudo systemctl disable --now halow-rssilog.service
  sudo rm /etc/systemd/system/halow-rssilog.service /home/alan/halow-rssilog.sh

Two tooling traps hit while setting this up, both worth avoiding:

- `pkill -f pirssilog` issued over SSH matches the SSH command line
  itself and kills the session that issued it. The command appears to do
  nothing at all — no output, no error. Build the pattern from a variable
  so the literal string is not in your own cmdline.
- busybox `ping` on the AP rejects fractional `-i` values, and busybox
  `nc` does not take the flags used for a banner grab; it prints usage,
  which reads like a successful empty result. Neither `arping` nor `od`
  nor `xxd` exists on that image.
