THE FOUR DIFFERENCES
================================================================

                    Raspberry Pi OS 6.6.51        OpenMANET 6.6.138
                    (2-bit skew, write fails)     (works)
  ----------------------------------------------------------------
  cs-gpios          <&gpio 8 1>, <&gpio 7 1>      <&gpio 8 1>
                    two entries                   one entry (property is
                                                  exactly 12 bytes)
  GPIO7             fe204000.spi ...              (MUX UNCLAIMED)
                    function gpio_out             (GPIO UNCLAIMED)
  reset-gpios       <&gpio 17 1>  (flag 1)        <&gpio 17 0>  (flag 0)
  spi-max-frequency 10 MHz                        50 MHz

Everything else that was compared is the same on both sides: MISO/MOSI/SCLK are
alt0, GPIO8 is gpio_out, and the spi0 node's dmas, clocks, interrupts, reg and
compatible properties match.

The clock difference is already eliminated -- RUN 4 of the Bookworm retest swept
400 kHz / 1 / 20 / 50 MHz through spi_clock_speed and the skew was identical at
every rate, 50 MHz included.

So what is left is the second chip select. The failing side registers two of
them and has the SPI controller holding GPIO7 as an output; the working side
registers one and never touches GPIO7. That variable had not been examined --
the earlier pinmux check (RUN 3) confirmed no pin was driven twice, which it
isn't, and stopped there.

Why it is worth testing rather than just noting: the number of chip selects is
not inert in this driver. One of the three rpi patches to spi-bcm2835.c that
OpenWrt imports is 950-0821, "spi-bcm2835: Support spi0-0cs and SPI_NO_CS mode",
so CS count has explicit special handling in exactly this file.

This is the first hypothesis in the whole investigation that comes from a
measured difference between a working and a failing configuration rather than
from reasoning about what might matter.

the same pass or immediately after, since that is the other unexplained delta.
