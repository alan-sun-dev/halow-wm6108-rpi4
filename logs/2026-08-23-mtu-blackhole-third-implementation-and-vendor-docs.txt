2026-08-23 (evening) — four results
===================================

1. A silent MTU black hole on the AP's bridge kills bulk uplink over HaLow.
   It is a property of the test network, not of the driver, and it is the
   likely cause of two things this repo had recorded as unexplained.
2. The HaLow link crosses one floor comfortably. Throughput measured, and
   the limit is channel width, not link quality.
3. A third SPI implementation (Heltec HT-HC01P on RPi 4) joined the bench.

4. Morse's official Linux Porting Guide says nothing about any of the
   three defects, which is why nobody following it would find them.


PART 1 — THE MTU BLACK HOLE
===========================

Symptom
-------

Bulk transfer over HaLow works in one direction and hangs in the other,
with no error anywhere. Small transfers work in both.

  size requested   uplink (Pi -> Mac)        downlink (Mac -> Pi)
  --------------   -----------------------   ---------------------
      1024 B       1024 B in  0.29 s         1024 B in  0.31 s
      8192 B       0 B in 34.71 s  HANG      8192 B in  0.51 s
     32768 B       0 B in 34.96 s  HANG      32768 B in 0.67 s
    524288 B       (never completed)         524288 B in 1.81 s

A trivial round trip (`ssh host echo hi`) completes in 0.56 s throughout,
so the link and the login are healthy. The failure threshold sits between
1 KiB and 8 KiB, which is a packet-size boundary, not a rate problem.

Cause
-----

  AP    br-lan  mtu 1460     eth0  mtu 1460     wlh0  mtu 1500
  Pi    wlan1   mtu 1500
  Mac   en5     mtu 1500

`wlh0` is 1500 but its bridge partner `eth0` is 1460, and a Linux bridge
takes the minimum of its ports, so `br-lan` is 1460. Frames larger than
1460 arriving from the HaLow side cannot be forwarded to the wired side
and the bridge drops them. A bridge is L2 and sends no ICMP
fragmentation-needed, so nothing tells either endpoint. Both ends
negotiated MSS from their own 1500-byte interfaces and neither ever learns
the path is smaller. TCP retransmits full-size segments forever.

Confirmed by a DF ping sweep:

  Pi -> AP (destination is the AP itself)   all sizes OK to a 1500-byte frame
  Mac -> Pi                                 OK at frame 1460, FAIL at 1468+

The Pi->AP direction passes at every size because the AP is the
destination: the frame is delivered locally on br-lan and never has to
egress `eth0`. The Mac->Pi DF failures are the *reply* being dropped, not
the request.

Fix, and confirmation
---------------------

One command on the station:

  ip link set wlan1 mtu 1460

Immediately after, with nothing else changed:

  uplink   8192 B  ->  8192 B in 0.32 s        (was 0 B in 34.7 s)
  uplink  65536 B  -> 65536 B in 0.45 s   1.164 Mbit/s
  uplink 262144 B  -> 262144 B in 1.42 s  1.480 Mbit/s
  downlink 512 KiB ->                     2.767 Mbit/s, md5 verified equal

To make it persist, on the station:

  nmcli connection modify halow 802-11-wireless.mtu 1460

The root cause is on the AP: nothing in `/etc/` sets 1460, `uci show
network` has no mtu option, and dmesg shows `br-lan` already at 1460 by
second 43.9 of boot — so a userspace daemon sets it after network config.
Candidates are openmanetd, mesh11sd and trelay, all installed. Attempting
to identify it from the openmanetd binary with `strings` failed its own
positive control (a token known to be in that program returned 0 hits), so
**which daemon sets it is not established**. Do not "fix" it by forcing
eth0 to 1500 until that is known: whatever sets it will likely set it
again at the next boot, producing an intermittent fault worse than the
current one.

A cleaner network-wide option, if more stations join: advertise the real
MTU over DHCP from the AP, `uci add_list dhcp.lan.dhcp_option='26,1460'`.

What this probably explains
---------------------------

Two entries recorded in NOTES as unexplained are exactly what a silent MTU
black hole produces:

- "uplink varies 4x, 0.23-0.90 Mbit/s" — bulk uplink stalling and
  recovering depending on how the transfer is shaped
- "the first sustained download truncated at 155648 of 4194304 bytes" — a
  TCP transfer that stops dead partway with no error

**Strongly indicated, not established** — neither event has been
reproduced with the MTU restored. Note also that the 5% ping loss is NOT
explained by this: small packets pass.

Also worth knowing: every throughput figure in this repo taken before this
was measured through the black hole.


PART 2 — RANGE AND THROUGHPUT ACROSS ONE FLOOR
==============================================

Continuing the range test. Position 3 is one floor up, no Ethernet, the
board on a battery, managed over its own Wi-Fi and over HaLow.

  position                   predicted     AP-side (30 samples)   station-side
  ------------------------   -----------   --------------------   ------------
  0.3 m, board to board       +0.7 dBm     -3   (-2..-4)           0 dBm
  2 m, line of sight         -15.8 dBm     -15.7 (-14..-19)        -12.1 dBm
  4 m, one wooden wall       -21.8 + wall  -29.9 (-28..-32)        -27.1 dBm
  one floor up                             -41.3 (-38..-44)        -37.7 dBm

Link quality at one floor: 60/60 pings, 0% loss.

A first ping run at this position reported 12% loss and was wrong: the
management SSH session was running over the same HaLow link at the time.
A clean run with nothing else on the link returned every one of 60
sequence numbers. Do not measure loss on a link you are also using as your
terminal.

Throughput, one floor up, after the MTU fix:

  downlink 2.77 Mbit/s      uplink 1.48 Mbit/s      RTT 8.3 ms

Why that is the ceiling, not a fault
------------------------------------

Under sustained load, the AP's rate controller sits at the top rate the
channel offers:

  2MHz SGI  C   MCS0  ... total 17 success / 56 attempts     <- probe rate
  2MHz SGI  A L MCS7  ... total 6111 success / 6750 attempts <- carrying the data

MCS7 is the highest MCS at 2 MHz, at 90.5% success. The 2 MHz MCS7 PHY
rate is about 6.5 Mbit/s, so 2.77 Mbit/s of TCP goodput is ~43%
efficiency, normal for 802.11.

The bandwidth is the limit and the regdomain allows more:

  phy: country SG   (920 - 925 @ 4), (N/A, 22)

5 MHz of spectrum, maximum channel width 4 MHz. The AP runs 2 MHz. 8 MHz
does not fit — that needs the US 902-928 MHz allocation.

External comparison (community.morsemicro.com thread 1124, castironclay,
~20 ft line of sight, tool not stated):

  1 MHz  0.82 Mbps      2 MHz  3.68 Mbps
  4 MHz  8.33 Mbps      8 MHz  5.16 Mbps  (they note both devices were
                                           "ever so slightly under powered")

Our 2.77 Mbit/s at 2 MHz against their 3.68 is the same ballpark; ours is
lower, plausibly because our SPI runs at 10 MHz where theirs runs at 50,
and because ours was TCP through SSH. Their 4 MHz figure is 2.3x their
2 MHz figure, which supports the expectation that moving this link to
4 MHz roughly doubles it. Their 8 MHz being *slower* than 4 MHz is a
caution against assuming wider is always better.

Headroom, for orientation: at -41 dBm with MCS0 sensitivity around
-95 dBm there is roughly 50 dB left at one floor.

Morse quote MM6108 at up to 32.3 Mbps. That is at 8 MHz, which this
regdomain does not permit, so it is not a comparable number. Morse's own
forum staff put SPI-attached hosts at "up to 21 Mbps with iperf" on their
EKH01 kit, and note that SPI throughput is dominated by host-side factors
(DMA, bus contention, clocks, power save). The driver has a bus throughput
profiler, `test_mode=6`, for separating bus capability from link
performance; `test_mode=4` is a chip reset and `test_mode=5` does block
reads and writes.


PART 3 — A THIRD SPI IMPLEMENTATION, AND A FIFTH DATA POINT
===========================================================

A Heltec HT-HC01P HAT on a Raspberry Pi 4B joined the bench, running
Heltec's factory image.

  image        OpenWrt 23.05.5, DISTRIB_DESCRIPTION "23.05.5 2.8.5-20251107"
  kernel       5.15.167
  board        Raspberry Pi 4 Model B Rev 1.4, serial 100000004dd92ccc
  eth0         e4:5f:01:40:8e:91      HaLow wlan0  0c:bf:74:40:8e:91
  driver       0-rel_1_15_3_2025_Apr_16      <- 1.15.3, NOT 2.8.5
  bcf          bcf_mf08551.bin
  management   10.42.0.1 on br-lan; ttyd on 7681 with NO authentication

**The 2.8.5 in the image name is Heltec's firmware package version, not
the Morse driver version.** The driver is 1.15.3 — older than the 2.0.1
this repo works with. An earlier note in this session that called it "much
newer than ours" was wrong.

Its device tree, read with `hexdump` (`od` is absent on that image; the
positive control for hexdump was run in the same breath):

  node                mm6108@0, compatible morse,mm610x-spi
  spi-max-frequency   02 fa f0 80  = 50,000,000
  reset-gpios         phandle 7, GPIO 5, flag 0
  cs-gpios            phandle 7, GPIO 8, flag 1

The comparison that matters
---------------------------

  |                  | this repo's station | OpenMANET AP | HT-HC01P | Morse EKH01 ref | MMECH06 (forum) |
  |------------------|---------------------|--------------|----------|-----------------|-----------------|
  | driver           | 2.0.1 + our fixes   | 2.0.1        | 1.15.3   | -               | 1.16.4          |
  | SPI clock        | **10 MHz**          | 50 MHz       | 50 MHz   | 50 MHz          | 50 MHz          |
  | reset-gpios flag | **1**               | 0            | 0        | **0**           | -               |
  | reset GPIO       | 17                  | 17           | 5        | 5               | 5               |
  | chip selects     | two (8, 7)          | one (8)      | one (8)  | one (8, flag 1) | one (8, flag 1) |

The fourth column is from Morse's own **official** Linux Porting Guide
(APPNOTE-24), section 6.1, the EKH01 EVK reference overlay:

   mm6108: mm6108@0 {
       compatible = "morse,mm610x-spi";
       reg = <0>;    /* CE0 */
       reset-gpios = <&gpio 5 0>;
       power-gpios = <&gpio 3 0>, <&gpio 7 0>;   /* WAKE, BUSY */
       spi-irq-gpios = <&gpio 25 0>;
       spi-max-frequency = <50000000>;
       status = "okay";
   };
   cs-gpios = <&gpio 8 1>;

So the pattern this repo found is not a coincidence across vendors. It is
**inherited from the official reference**: 50 MHz, and a `reset-gpios`
flag of 0. Those two settings are exactly what hides defect 3 (the delay
model only lands on a working value at 50 MHz) and defect 2 (flag 0 means
`gpiod_set_value(reset, 1)` drives the pin high, so RESET_N never fires
and the chip is never knocked out of SPI mode).

The property table in the same document describes `reset-gpios` only as
"GPIO descriptor connected to the MM6108 RESET line" and **says nothing
about polarity**.


PART 4 — WHAT THE OFFICIAL PORTING GUIDE DOES NOT SAY
======================================================

MM_APPNOTE-24 "Linux Porting Guide" v2, 22 pages. Keyword counts over the
extracted text, with positive controls in the same pass (Morse 115 hits,
SPI 21 hits, so the search works):

  chip select / chip-select        0
  deassert                         0
  74                               0
  init sequence / initialisation   0
  training                         0
  delay / inter-block              0
  inter-transaction                0
  CMD53 / CMD63                    0
  probe fail / troubleshoot        0

The guide covers kernel patching, driver compilation, firmware binaries,
hostapd and wpa_supplicant, the four device tree properties, bring-up
commands and the test_mode table. **It has no troubleshooting section and
documents none of the low-level SPI behaviour that this repo's three
defects concern.**

That matters for the upstream case. The requirement that the chip needs
~74 clocks with chip select *deasserted* is stated publicly only in a
forum thread (community.morsemicro.com/t/i-mx93-spi-driver-for-mm6108/506),
which this repo already cites. Someone porting by the official document
has no way to learn it, and the released driver does not implement it
correctly.

A separate claim from the community build thread (topic 1124), verbatim:

  "From 1.15.3, patching the kernel is practically required, so sticking
   as close to one of these versions as possible will make integration
   significantly smoother."

The stated reasons are mesh networking, channel switch announcements and
SPI support, and the recipe cherry-picks a whole Morse kernel branch
(`morse/mm/rpi-6.12.21/1.16.x`).

**This repo is a counter-example for the infrastructure-mode case**:
stock Raspberry Pi OS 6.6.51, an unpatched kernel, driver 2.0.1 plus the
three `spi.c` fixes in `patches/upstream/`, and the link associates with
WPA3-SAE, runs DHCP, moves 4 MiB checksummed in both directions and
reports `errors 0`. Mesh and CSA were never exercised here, so the claim
is not refuted in general — but for a station on 6.6.51 the kernel patches
are not required, and that is measured, not argued.

Nobody in that thread reports the failure signature this repo chased
(`c0 7f`, CMD63 -71, `SPI_NO_CS`, `spi_inter_block_delay_bytes`), which is
consistent with everyone running the reference 50 MHz and flag 0.


PART 5 — rsn_beacon_mode: WHY A 1.15.3 STATION CANNOT SEE THIS AP'S SECURITY
============================================================================

While configuring the HT-HC01P as a station of the OpenMANET AP, it would
not associate. It scanned, found the AP at -52 dBm, and never attempted
authentication. hostapd on the AP never logged its MAC at all.

The supplicant's own view named the problem:

  bssid              frequency  signal  flags        ssid
  3c:1a:cc:70:3f:ca  5785       -51     [WEP][ESS]   BCM2711-57e7

`[WEP]` means the privacy bit is set but no RSN element could be parsed.
A network block requiring `key_mgmt=SAE` cannot match that, so
wpa_supplicant stays in `wpa_state=SCANNING` forever and never tries.

The same AP, at the same time, seen by the two stations:

  this repo's station, driver 2.0.1     RSN: Version 1, Group CCMP,
                                        Pairwise CCMP,
                                        Authentication suites: SAE
  HT-HC01P, driver 1.15.3               capability: ESS Privacy (0x0011)
                                        and no RSN element at all

Both `country=SG`, both with 5785 MHz [157] at 22 dBm available and not
passive-scan. Counts were taken with positive controls (capability and
SSID both returned 1 on the HC01P, so the scan and the grep were working
and the RSN 0 was meaningful).

Why the RSN is missing from the beacon, from `beacon.c` in 2.0.1:

  /* Set RSN IE in beacon mode */
  static enum morse_mac_rsn_beacon_mode
  rsn_beacon_mode __read_mostly = RSN_BEACON_DISABLED;
  MODULE_PARM_DESC(rsn_beacon_mode,
      "Mode for insertion of RSN IEs in beacons (none, long beacons only, or all beacons)");

  enum morse_mac_rsn_beacon_mode {
      RSN_BEACON_DISABLED = 0x00,   /* default */
      RSN_BEACON_LONG     = 0x01,
      RSN_BEACON_ALL      = 0x02
  };

S1G beacons omit the RSN IE by default; a station is expected to learn the
security configuration from probe responses. Driver 2.0.1 surfaces it,
1.15.3 does not.

On the AP the parameter is a uci option, not a sysfs file — the driver is
built into the OpenMANET kernel and `/sys/module/morse` does not exist
there. It is listed in `MM_MOD_INT` in `/lib/netifd/wireless/morse.sh`:

  uci set wireless.radio1.rsn_beacon_mode='2'
  uci commit wireless && wifi reload

Applied and confirmed in the AP's boot-time parameter dump:

  morse_spi spi0.0:     rsn_beacon_mode                         : 2

and confirmed on air — this repo's station now sees the RSN element in
the beacon rather than only in probe responses.

**Unresolved.** The HT-HC01P still did not associate in the three minutes
after the change, and hostapd still logged nothing from its MAC (positive
control: 46 hostapd lines present, so logging works). Whether its
supplicant needs a kick, or 1.15.3 cannot use the RSN IE even when it is
in the beacon, is not known — diagnosing it needs access to that box,
which was unavailable at the time because the laptop's USB-Ethernet was
cabled to the AP instead.

A related correction: the AP's driver version is
`0-rel_mm6108_2_0_1_2026_Jun_11`, the same 2.0.1 this repo patches. So the
station comparison above varies driver version and nothing else.

SECURITY NOTE ON THE HT-HC01P
=============================

As shipped, that image runs `ttyd` on `0.0.0.0:7681` with **no
authentication** — `/token` returns `{"token": ""}` and the page loads
without a challenge. It is bridged into `br-lan` along with `eth0` and the
Pi's built-in 5 GHz AP, and the firewall's lan zone is `input ACCEPT`.
It also runs LuCI on 80/443, `dppd` on 8080 and `mediamtx` on 554/9997,
and its dnsmasq serves DHCP on lan with no `ignore` flag.

Anyone within range of its Wi-Fi AP who knows the factory password gets a
root shell with no credentials. Do not put its Ethernet on the house
switch without first disabling the DHCP server, setting a root password
and dealing with ttyd.

Tooling note for that image: `od`, `dtc`, `fdtget` and `wpa_cli` are
absent; `hexdump`, `xxd`, `strings` and `wpa_cli_s1g` are present. Check
before believing an empty read.
